❓ Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before investing in mutual funds through eNivesh.

What is a Mutual Fund and how does it work? ▾
A mutual fund pools money from many investors and professionally manages it through an Asset Management Company (AMC). The fund invests this capital in stocks, bonds, or other assets as per its stated objective. Each investor owns units proportional to their contribution, sharing in the fund's gains and losses. Returns are not guaranteed and are market-linked.
What is SIP and how is it different from Lumpsum investing? ▾
A SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) lets you invest a fixed amount at regular intervals (monthly/weekly), benefiting from rupee cost averaging and financial discipline. A Lumpsum is a one-time investment of a larger amount — ideal when you have surplus capital and markets are at attractive valuations. SIP is recommended for salaried investors; lumpsum suits those with large windfall amounts.
Are Mutual Fund returns guaranteed? What are the risks? ▾
No, mutual fund returns are NOT guaranteed. Equity funds are subject to market risks and can fluctuate significantly. Historically, equity mutual funds have delivered 10–15% CAGR over 10+ year periods. Debt funds carry lower risk with lower returns. Always match the fund category to your risk appetite and investment horizon before investing.
What is an NFO (New Fund Offer)? Should I invest in one? ▾
An NFO is the initial launch period of a new mutual fund scheme where units are sold at ₹10/unit. NFOs are useful if the fund covers a unique strategy or theme not available in existing funds. However, a lower NAV does not mean lower cost — always evaluate the fund's investment objective and AMC track record before investing in an NFO.
What is ELSS and how does it save taxes? ▾
ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme) is a type of equity mutual fund qualifying for tax deductions under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. You can claim up to ₹1.5 lakh per financial year. ELSS has a mandatory 3-year lock-in period — the shortest among all Section 80C instruments — and offers market-linked returns.
What is NAV and what is expense ratio in mutual funds? ▾
NAV (Net Asset Value) is the per-unit price of a mutual fund, calculated daily. A higher NAV doesn't mean the fund is expensive — returns depend on NAV growth, not the absolute level. Expense Ratio is the annual fee charged by the AMC, expressed as a % of AUM. Lower expense ratio = more returns for you. Direct plans have lower expense ratios (0.5–1%) versus regular plans (1.5–2.5%).
How do I redeem my Mutual Fund investments? ▾
For open-ended funds, you can redeem anytime (subject to exit load). Submit a redemption request online — proceeds are credited to your bank account within 1–3 business days for equity funds and same/next day for liquid funds. ELSS funds have a mandatory 3-year lock-in and cannot be redeemed before that period.

What is the minimum amount to start a SIP or Lumpsum investment? ▾
SIPs can be started with as little as ₹100–₹500 per month, depending on the fund. Lumpsum investments typically require a minimum of ₹500–₹5,000. There is no maximum limit. eNivesh recommends starting early even with small amounts — the power of compounding over time is significant.
